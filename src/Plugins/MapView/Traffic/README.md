# Traffic

Map view plugin: ADS-B traffic and airspaces around the current unit (or the map center when the unit has no position).

- **ADS-B**: [adsb.fi](https://adsb.fi) open data, `point` request every second, no key. On HTTP 429 or 503 the requests back off (`Retry-After` when given, otherwise doubling up to 2 min). A 200 reply without the aircraft list (a captive portal or proxy page) is reported as `ADS-B: bad reply` and the aircraft on the map are kept. Aircraft are drawn on the map as icons by the ADS-B emitter category (airplane, helicopter, balloon, parachutist, UAV, ground vehicle, obstacle) rotated by track. Hovering an icon shows the info panel (callsign and ICAO hex, category and type, baro altitude, vertical speed, the altitude above the unit, speed, distance and bearing), a click pins it to the aircraft and highlights the aircraft in the panel and on the radar (click again to unpin). All altitudes are in metres: `BARO` is the pressure altitude (std 1013), `GNSS` is the geometric altitude above the WGS84 ellipsoid when the aircraft reports it; the instruments panel lists both, the map info panel shows the baro altitude only. Aircraft missing from a reply (or with a position older than 60 s) are removed at once, and so are aircraft not updated for 30 s (request errors, backoff). Colors are by the conflict prediction, computed on every reply: the possible positions of every aircraft (a slice of its reach volume) are sampled over the `horizon` (the tools menu item, 60 s by default, 10...600) in 2 s steps, and an aircraft is a threat when its possible positions come closer than the separation (500 m horizontally and 150 m vertically) to the possible positions of the unit: red - within 30 s, yellow - within the horizon, cyan - other (white in the panel); `Tau` is the time to that first instant. Two outlines are closer than the separation when one is inside the other, when their edges cross or when a vertex of one is closer than that to an edge of the other. A reach slice at time t is the set of positions reachable at constant ground speed with the turn limited by the bank angle of the ADS-B emitter category (omega = g·tan(bank)/v, capped at 6°/s; the outer boundary is the full-rate turn for a part of the time then straight, Dubins style), and the altitude band is the reported vertical speed ± a per-category change, plus 50 m of reference uncertainty. Hover-capable categories (rotorcraft, UAV, balloon, parachutist) and aircraft with no reported track get the full disc of their speed instead. The aircraft altitude is always the ADS-B baro altitude (every transponder reports it; the GNSS altitude only when there is no baro), the vertical speed is the reported `baro_rate` (`geom_rate` when there is no baro one). The unit altitude is the pressure altitude from `sns.baro.mbar` (`PA`) when the unit reports it (the pressure is above zero), `HMSL` from `est.pos.hmsl` otherwise; an unknown altitude counts as the same altitude. When the unit has no position the map center is the origin for the distance and bearing only: there are no alerts and the list is not sorted (the reply order). Aircraft on the ground and the own aircraft never raise an alert. The Traffic instruments panel (button next to the Terminal) lists the aircraft (category icon, altitudes, distance, bearing, `Tau`: seconds to the yellow zone, `---` is never) threats first by the time, then the rest nearest first (while the unit has a position); the header shows the unit altitude in use; a click highlights the aircraft with a thin white frame (in the panel, on the radar and on the map; click again to drop; not the own aircraft) and centers the map on it; a panel pinned on the map is unpinned (the info panel is pinned by a click on the map only).
- **Radar**: a TCAS-like widget in the main layout (bottom right, like Signals) with the `radar` button in the widgets bar while the plugin is enabled: the unit, heading up (north up with the map center as the origin), range rings, the red and yellow zone rings and a compass arc. The aircraft are drawn by distance and bearing as a red square, a yellow circle or a white diamond (the aircraft on the ground are not drawn: the ground traffic of an airport would be a cluster at the unit) with the altitude above the unit in m and the climb/descent arrow, the threats also with the time to the conflict in s, and a blue arrow of the track (the red and yellow rings are a distance reference, the alerts are by the reach slices above). The aircraft under the compass band (the outer ring with the numbers) are not drawn, the scale is kept. Without the unit position the radar shows `NO UNIT` (the unit altitude in use is in the header of the instruments panel). View: all around (the unit in the center, full rings) or the sector ahead (the unit at the bottom, the rings are the half circles ahead clipped by the edges, the compass arc spans ±55°), the `360` and `SECT` buttons at the bottom (kept in the settings). The range (the outer ring, 1...400 km, apart from the request `radius`) is edited in the field at the top right of the radar (Enter applies, Esc reverts) and kept in the settings. A click on an aircraft highlights it (as in the panel) and centers the map on it, a click on the unit symbol centers the map on the unit.
- **3D view**: a separate window (the `view3d` item in the tools menu) with the traffic in 3D around the unit: the sea level grid (y = 0) with the range rings up to the request `radius`, every airborne aircraft (the aircraft on the ground are not drawn) at its true altitude with the velocity vector, the altitude stem and a label, and the reach volumes of the unit and of the threats: the slices of the possible positions over the `horizon`, each slice its altitude band (the top and the bottom ring joined by the corner edges), lofted by the corner edges; on every reply a wave (the live slice, a filled box) sweeps the horizon in about a second and turns red from the moment the slices of the aircraft and of the unit intersect. The positions are extrapolated between the replies by the reported speed and track and smoothed, so the scene moves continuously. Drawn with a software projection on a Canvas (no QtQuick3D dependency) painted in a worker thread: a frame waits for the previous paint and the canvas resolution drops to 0.75 of the window (scaled up by the scene graph) while a paint takes longer than 50 ms, back to full when it takes less than 20 ms, so a big window does not lag; the aircraft symbols are drawn over the volumes and the labels are native items over the canvas, sharp at any canvas resolution; orbit camera (drag, wheel zoom, pan), vertical scale toggle. The window closes when the plugin is disabled.
- **Airspaces**: CTR, TMA, R, D, P ... from the [OpenAIP](https://www.openaip.net) country export (`<cc>_asp.geojson`, no key) as outlines with name and limits. The country file is downloaded once and kept in the storage next to the maps cache (`Documents/UAVOS/Storage/airspaces/openaip/<cc>.geojson`, the `cache` item in the menu shows the files count and size), after that it is loaded from there without the network (delete the file to download it again); parsing runs in a worker thread, the map shows the airspaces within 1.5 radius of the origin and follows the origin as it moves.

Tools menu: `use` turns the plugin on and off and brings the panel to front, `radius` in km, `horizon` of the conflict prediction in s, `country` of the airspaces (`Off` is off), `view3d` opens the 3D window. The radar modes are switched in the widget (see below). Our own aircraft as seen by ADS-B (within 25 m of the unit on every axis, an unknown altitude counts as the same) is not drawn on the map, is a grey row in the panel and never raises an alert. While the plugin is off its panel and button are not in the instruments row. Request errors are shown in the panel header and in the menu item description as `ADS-B: HTTP 429`.

**Offline**: when the OS reports the network unreachable or a request fails below HTTP (no route, DNS, timeout) the panel shows `OFFLINE` instead of the list and the menu item description reads `OFFLINE`. ADS-B keeps probing every 5 s (5 s timeout), the first reply clears the state. The airspaces download is tried once (5 s timeout): without a connection the `country` item reads `Offline, cached countries only` and its list is reduced to the countries with a file in the cache, which still load; the full list and the downloads are back when `use` is switched off and on.
