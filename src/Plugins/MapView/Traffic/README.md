# Traffic

Map view plugin: ADS-B traffic and airspaces around the current unit (or the map center when the unit has no position).

- **ADS-B**: [adsb.fi](https://adsb.fi) open data, `point` request every second, no key. On HTTP 429 or 503 the requests back off (`Retry-After` when given, otherwise doubling up to 2 min). A 200 reply without the aircraft list (a captive portal or proxy page) is reported as `ADS-B: bad reply` and the aircraft on the map are kept. Aircraft are drawn on the map as icons by the ADS-B emitter category (airplane, helicopter, balloon, parachutist, UAV, ground vehicle, obstacle) rotated by track. Hovering an icon shows the info panel (callsign and ICAO hex, category and type, altitude, vertical speed, speed, distance and bearing), a click pins it to the aircraft and highlights the aircraft in the panel and on the radar (click again to unpin). All altitudes are in metres: `GNSS` is the geometric altitude above the WGS84 ellipsoid when the aircraft reports it, `BARO` is the pressure altitude otherwise; the panel shows both when available and labels which one is used. Aircraft missing from a reply (or with a position older than 60 s) are removed at once, and so are aircraft not updated for 30 s (request errors, backoff). Colors are by the time to the alert zone around the unit (TCAS-like), computed on every reply from the relative position and velocity (distance and bearing, ground speed and track, vertical speed of both) over the cone of the possible maneuvers of the aircraft (the worst case of the track within ±1500/speed deg, 10...60, and the vertical speed within ±150/speed m/s, 1...5: a fast aircraft can not turn or climb as sharply): red - inside 2 km and 300 m by altitude, or there within 30 s; yellow - inside 4 km and 600 m, or there within 60 s; cyan - other (white in the panel). The unit altitude is the pressure altitude from `sns.baro.mbar` (`PA`, compared with the ADS-B baro altitude) when the unit reports it (the pressure is above zero), `HMSL` from GNSS (compared with the GNSS altitude) otherwise; an unknown altitude counts as the same altitude. When the unit has no position the map center is the origin for the distance and bearing only: there are no alerts and the list is not sorted (the reply order). Aircraft on the ground and the own aircraft never raise an alert. The Traffic instruments panel (button next to the Terminal) lists the aircraft (category icon, altitudes, distance, bearing, `Tau`: seconds to the yellow zone, `---` is never) threats first by the time, then the rest nearest first (while the unit has a position); the header shows the unit altitude in use; a click highlights the aircraft with a thin white frame (in the panel, on the radar and on the map; click again to drop; not the own aircraft) and centers the map on it; a panel pinned on the map is unpinned (the info panel is pinned by a click on the map only).
- **Radar**: a TCAS-like widget in the main layout (bottom right, like Signals) with the `radar` button in the widgets bar while the plugin is enabled: the unit, heading up (north up with the map center as the origin), range rings, the red and yellow zone rings and a compass arc. The aircraft are drawn by distance and bearing as a red square, a yellow circle or a white diamond (the aircraft on the ground are not drawn: the ground traffic of an airport would be a cluster at the unit) with the altitude above the unit in m and the climb/descent arrow, the threats also with the time to the zone in s, and a blue arrow of the track. The aircraft under the compass band (the outer ring with the numbers) are not drawn, the scale is kept. Without the unit position the radar shows `NO UNIT` (the unit altitude in use is in the header of the instruments panel). View: all around (the unit in the center, full rings) or the sector ahead (the unit at the bottom, the rings are the half circles ahead clipped by the edges, the compass arc spans ±55°), the `360` and `SECT` buttons at the bottom (kept in the settings). The range (the outer ring, 1...400 km, apart from the request `radius`) is edited in the field at the top right of the radar (Enter applies, Esc reverts) and kept in the settings. A click on an aircraft highlights it (as in the panel) and centers the map on it, a click on the unit symbol centers the map on the unit.
- **Airspaces**: CTR, TMA, R, D, P ... from the [OpenAIP](https://www.openaip.net) country export (`<cc>_asp.geojson`, no key) as outlines with name and limits. The country file is downloaded every 10 min and parsed in a worker thread, the map shows the airspaces within 1.5 radius of the origin and follows the origin as it moves.

Tools menu: `use` turns the plugin on and off and brings the panel to front, `radius` in km, `country` of the airspaces (`Off` is off), The radar modes are switched in the widget (see below). Our own aircraft as seen by ADS-B (within 25 m of the unit on every axis, an unknown altitude counts as the same) is not drawn on the map, is a grey row in the panel and never raises an alert. While the plugin is off its panel and button are not in the instruments row. Request errors are shown in the panel header and in the menu item description as `ADS-B: HTTP 429`.

**Offline**: when the OS reports the network unreachable or a request fails below HTTP (no route, DNS, timeout) the panel shows `OFFLINE` instead of the list and the menu item description reads `OFFLINE`. ADS-B keeps probing every 5 s (5 s timeout), the first reply clears the state. The airspaces download is tried once (5 s timeout): without a connection the `country` item reads `Offline, restart the plugin to retry` and is locked until `use` is switched off and on.
